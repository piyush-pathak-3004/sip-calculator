Complete Guide: Step-Up SIP with Lumpsum, 10Y SIP + 10Y Wait & Inflation-Adjusted SWP

1. Why Combine Lumpsum with a Step-Up SIP?

Most investors have an existing savings buffer (lumpsum) plus monthly cash flow from their salary. By deploying your available lumpsum on Day 1 and pairing it with an annual Step-Up SIP (increasing contributions by 10% every year as your salary grows), your portfolio benefits from both early compounding and rising capital inflows.

2. The 10-Year SIP + 10-Year Wait Strategy (Coast FIRE)

What happens if you invest ₹10,000/month with a 10% annual step-up for 10 years (plus ₹1 Lakh lumpsum) at 12% return, and then stop contributing for the next 10 years? By Year 10, your total out-of-pocket investment is ₹20.12 Lakhs and your corpus is ₹37.04 Lakhs. Over the next 10 years with zero new SIPs, that ₹37.04 Lakhs compounds into ₹1.22 Crore by Year 20.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How is Step-Up SIP calculated each year?

In a compounding Step-Up SIP, Year 1 uses your base monthly SIP. In Year 2, the monthly SIP increases by your step-up percentage or fixed rupee amount. Inside each year, compounding happens monthly using the monthly rate (Annual Return ÷ 12).

How much corpus do I need for ₹50,000/month expense after 20 years?

At 6% annual inflation, ₹50,000/month today becomes ₹1,60,357/month in 20 years. To sustain that inflation-indexed withdrawal for 25 years in retirement (assuming 9% post-retirement return), you need approximately ₹2.9 Crore to ₹3.2 Crore at retirement.
